## Standards and guidance (official bodies)

- [ASTM International](https://www.astm.org) — the US standards body that publishes ASTM C143
  (slump of hydraulic-cement concrete) and ASTM C39 (compressive strength of cylindrical
  specimens), both cited by number in the lab manual. Students can read the
  official scope and precision statements of each test method here before running
  the test; first-year
  students and lab instructors both benefit. Standard texts are paid — only the free summaries
  are used in the course materials.
- [American Concrete Institute](https://www.concrete.org) — publishes ACI codes, reports, and
  certification programmes for concrete construction. Suitable for advanced learners preparing
  design-oriented coursework; its free publication previews show how testing practice
  connects to specification writing.
- [American Cement Association](https://www.cement.org) — the research and education body for
  North American cement manufacturers (formerly the Portland Cement Association). Its free
  technical publications on cement types and durability give instructors verified background
  reading for the cement chemistry lectures; specific pages work well as pre-lab reading.

## Open courseware

- [MIT OpenCourseWare — Civil and Environmental Engineering](https://ocw.mit.edu/search/?d=Civil%20and%20Environmental%20Engineering) —
  over one hundred complete MIT courses with lecture notes and assignments, free to browse.
  For advanced learners: the problem-set styles of the materials-related courses are a useful
  model when designing tutorial sheets on microstructure and mix design.
- [NPTEL](https://nptel.ac.in) — the Indian national platform of video courses recorded by
  institute professors, including several concrete technology offerings under the Civil
  Engineering domain (browse Domains → Civil Engineering). Ideal for first-year students who
  want a lecture-series companion; specific videos are worth pointing to week by week so
  students hear a second explanation of curing and workability.

## Videos and simulations

- [Practical Engineering (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/@PracticalEngineeringChannel) — a
  well-produced channel demonstrating civil engineering concepts with physical models, including
  concrete and materials behaviour. Suits first-year students; the relevant episode works well
  screened before the slump lab so students visualise failure modes before touching wet concrete.

## Supporting skills for the laboratory

- [Khan Academy — Statistics and Probability](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/statistics-probability) —
  sixteen self-paced units covering display of quantitative data, distributions, and hypothesis
  testing. This is the direct prerequisite for the control-chart and characteristic-strength
  calculations in [`code/concrete_analysis/`](../code/concrete_analysis/); the
  "displaying data" unit is the suggested warm-up before students plot their first strength
  histogram.
- The Python stack used by the analysis scripts (pandas, Matplotlib, SciPy) is listed with
  verified links on the [tools page](tools.md), and the four country-standard Colab notebooks
  are one click away in [`code/notebooks/`](../code/notebooks/).
