# Concrete Technology — Outcomes-to-Assessment Map

The matrix below shows which assessment instruments carry the evidence for each course
outcome, with indicative weight contributions (percentages of the final grade). This is the
map used for course-file reviews and accreditation visits; every outcome is assessed at
least twice, and the laboratory reports bridge cognitive and psychomotor domains.

| Course outcome | Quizzes (10%) | Lab reports (20%) | Midterm (20%) | Project (15%) | Final (35%) | Evidence level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CO1 Cement composition & hydration | Q1, Q2 | - | 5% | 2% | 7% | Understand |
| CO2 Fresh properties & workability | Q3 | 8% (slump report) | 4% | 2% | 6% | Apply |
| CO3 Mix proportioning | Q4 | - | 6% | 8% | 7% | Apply / Analyse |
| CO4 Strength testing & acceptance | Q5 | 12% (cube report) | 5% | 3% | 9% | Apply / Evaluate |
| CO5 Durability mechanisms | - | - | - | - | 6% | Understand / Evaluate |

## How to read the map

- Empty cells mean the instrument does not primarily target that outcome; small weights
  indicate incidental coverage through integrated problems.
- The strongest evidence for CO4 is the cube-strength laboratory report combined with the
  quality-control quiz and the final examination's acceptance-rule problem.
- The mix-design project is the only instrument that integrates CO2-CO4 in one deliverable,
  which is why its rubric (see `assessments/grading-rubric-template.md`) emphasises
  methodology and justified adjustment over the final numbers.

## Continuous improvement loop

After each semester: (1) compute the mean score per outcome from the gradebook, (2) flag any
outcome below 60% class average, (3) adjust the corresponding week's tutorial activity the
next term, and (4) record the action in the course file. This closes the
outcomes-assessment-improvement loop expected by most accreditation frameworks.
