# Lecture 2 Outline — Fresh Concrete: Workability and the Slump Test

**Duration:** 50 minutes · **Format:** lecture + live demonstration ·
**Course:** Concrete Technology · **Week:** 5 (fresh concrete block)

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to: (1) define workability and distinguish it from consistency;
(2) perform and interpret the slump test per their national standard (ASTM C143 or
BS EN 12350-2 family); (3) classify slump into S1-S5 (or local target ranges) and connect
each class to a placement scenario; and (4) identify at least three factors that change
workability without changing water content.

## Slide deck (titles and key points)

1. **What is workability?**
   - Ease of placing, compacting, finishing without segregation
   - Consistency vs stability vs compactability
2. **The slump test step by step**
   - 300 mm cone, three lifts of equal depth, 25 rods, 5 s lift
   - True slump / shear slump / collapse slump - what to record and what to redo
3. **Slump classes around the world**
   - EN 206: S1 10-40 ... S5 >= 220 mm; ASTM: report to 5 mm, no classes
   - Interactive: match six site photos to a class
4. **Workability is context-dependent**
   - Pumped concrete, congested reinforcement, slipforming, pavements
5. **What moves slump without adding water**
   - Superplasticizers, grading, particle shape, temperature, time (slump loss)
6. **Live demo**
   - Two mini cones: dry-ish mix vs over-sanded mix; measure and classify
7. **Safety and QA framing**
   - Why adding water on site is the classic quality crime; W/C integrity
8. **Wrap-up poll**
   - One-minute paper: describe a project where you would specify S2 vs S4

## In-class activities

- Live demonstration (10 minutes) with a student recorder entering values into the lab data
  sheet template from `lab-manual/`.
- Photo-matching exercise in pairs; answers argued verbally, not graded.

## Assessment hook

This lecture is the direct preparation for Laboratory Report 1 (slump test). The lab data
sheet must include the true/shear/collapse judgement, and the report asks students to
defend the class they assigned using the standard's tolerance bands.

## Common misconceptions to address

- "Higher slump always means better concrete" - workability must fit the placement method.
- "The slump number is the property" - it measures consistency, one component of workability.
