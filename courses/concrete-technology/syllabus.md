## Course description

Concrete is the most widely used construction material on earth, and this course develops a
working understanding of why. Students follow concrete across its full life cycle: cement
chemistry and hydration, fresh-concrete rheology and workability testing, mix proportioning,
hardened-state strength development, quality control, and the durability mechanisms that
limit service life. The course pairs every major concept with a hands-on or computational
activity, culminating in a laboratory programme built around the slump test and the cube
compressive strength test, analysed with open Python tooling.

## Course outcomes (CO)

By the end of the course, a successful student can:

- **CO1** Explain Portland cement composition, the hydration reaction and how it drives
  setting, strength gain and heat evolution.
- **CO2** Measure and interpret fresh-concrete properties (workability, slump classes) and
  relate them to placement conditions.
- **CO3** Perform a preliminary concrete mix proportioning using a published method and
  justify each adjustment.
- **CO4** Conduct compressive strength testing, compute specimen strength and
  characteristic strength, and apply acceptance rules from a national standard.
- **CO5** Discuss durability mechanisms (carbonation, chloride ingress, sulfate attack) and
  identify design and construction measures that mitigate them.

## Learning resources

- Original lecture outlines and notes provided in this repository (no textbook copying).
- Any recent edition of a standard concrete technology text may be used for background
  reading; the syllabus is self-contained.
- National standard family relevant to the class (ASTM / BS EN / IS / BDS) as assigned.
- Python 3.9+ with NumPy, pandas and matplotlib for the computational activities.

## Policies

Attendance is expected at all laboratory sessions for safety reasons. Late lab reports lose
5% per day up to five days. Academic integrity follows institutional policy; data reported
in lab work must be measured or clearly labelled as synthetic.
