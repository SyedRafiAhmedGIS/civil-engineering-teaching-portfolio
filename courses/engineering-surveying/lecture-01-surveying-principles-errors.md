# Lecture 1 Outline — Principles of Surveying and Sources of Error

**Duration:** 50 minutes · **Format:** lecture + booking drill ·
**Course:** Engineering Surveying · **Week:** 1-2 (foundations block)

## Learning objectives

Students will be able to: (1) distinguish plane from geodetic surveying and state when each
is appropriate; (2) classify observations into blunders, systematic and random errors with
surveying examples; (3) apply the working-from-whole-to-part principle to a simple site
plan; and (4) book a measurement to the correct precision with the right significant
figures.

## Slide deck (titles and key points)

1. **Why surveying decides whether the bridge meets the road**
   - Opening case: a 30 cm setting-out error and what it cost
2. **The two big principles**
   - Work from whole to part; check from independent observation
3. **Plane vs geodetic**
   - Earth curvature: when can we ignore it? (1 cm in 10 km hint)
4. **Every measurement is wrong - by how much?**
   - Blunders (count the tape lengths), systematic (tape too short, collimation),
     random (spread around a mean)
5. **The error toolbox**
   - Correction formulas preview, redundancy, closure as the built-in check
6. **Booking discipline**
   - Field book conventions; no erasers; single-line strike-through + initials
7. **Booking drill (in class)**
   - Six taped distances booked on a projected form; class votes on the correct entries
8. **Wrap-up**
   - One-minute paper: classify five given observations

## In-class activities

- Booking drill (10 minutes) using the projected field-book form; results debated, not
  graded - the graded version happens in the week 2 practical.
- Think-pair-share: "which error type survives averaging?" (systematic - key insight).

## Assessment hook

Quiz 1 is a short classification + booking question in the same format as the drill.
The semester's grading rubric (see `assessments/`) allocates marks for booking discipline
in every field notebook check.

## Common misconceptions to address

- "Precise means accurate" - a micrometer can measure the wrong thing precisely.
- "Random errors can be eliminated" - they can only be reduced and bounded.
