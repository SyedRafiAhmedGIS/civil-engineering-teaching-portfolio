## Course description

Every built asset starts with measurements, and surveying is the discipline that makes those
measurements trustworthy. This course introduces the measurement of distance, elevation and
angle; the instruments that acquire them (tape, level, theodolite, total station, GNSS); and
the error models that turn raw observations into defensible results. Students plan and run a
closed traverse, reduce a level run, contour a small site, and set out a simple structure,
recording everything in field books kept to professional standards.

## Course outcomes (CO)

- **CO1** State the principles of surveying, classify errors, and apply corrections to
  basic distance and elevation measurements.
- **CO2** Conduct a levelling run (rise-and-fall and height-of-instrument methods), compute
  misclosure, and distribute the error acceptably.
- **CO3** Operate a total station to measure horizontal/vertical angles and distances, and
  run a closed traverse with angular and linear adjustments.
- **CO4** Produce and interpret contour plans, and compute areas and volumes for earthwork.
- **CO5** Explain the fundamentals of GNSS positioning and set out points/lines/grades for
  construction, including a method statement and check regime.

- Spreadsheet or Python for reductions; template columns shown in the weekly plan.

## Policies

Field sessions run in most weather; bring appropriate clothing. Safety vests are mandatory
near roads. Data fabrication ends careers - record what the instrument shows, flag suspect
readings, and repeat the measurement. Late field-book checks attract the same penalty as
late reports (5% per day, up to five days).
