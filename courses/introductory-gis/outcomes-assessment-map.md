# Introductory GIS — Outcomes-to-Assessment Map

| Course outcome | Lab exercises (25%) | Quizzes (10%) | Project (25%) | Midterm (15%) | Final (25%) | Evidence level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CO1 What a GIS is / geospatial thinking | Lab 1 | Q1 | 2% | 4% | 5% | Understand |
| CO2 Vector vs raster models | Lab 2, 3 | Q2 | 3% | 4% | 5% | Understand / Apply |
| CO3 CRS handling | Lab 4, 5 | Q3 | 4% | 4% | 5% | Apply / Analyse |
| CO4 Data acquisition & joins | Lab 6, 7 | Q4 | 5% | 3% | 5% | Apply |
| CO5 Spatial analysis & map communication | Lab 8, 9 | Q5 | 11% | - | 5% | Apply / Evaluate / Create |

## Design notes

- CO5 carries the largest single evidence block (the project's analysis + final map), which
  is deliberate: GIS competence is demonstrated by a defensible analysis communicated
  honestly, not by button sequences.
- CRS handling (CO3) is re-tested in the project: every map must be in a stated, correct CRS
  and the project report must justify the choice - the classic failure mode is caught twice.
- The project rubric reuses the shared grading rubric template with GIS-specific criteria
  (data sourcing, CRS justification, cartographic communication).

## Continuous improvement loop

Lab completion rates and per-outcome averages are reviewed each semester. The week 1
no-install demo exists because previous cohorts installed software slowly; if week 4 quiz
averages on CRS stay low, week 5 gains an extra drill rather than new content.
